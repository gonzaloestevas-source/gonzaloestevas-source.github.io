Gust load alleviation for a flexible blended-wing-body aircraft

Individual Research Project · Cranfield University · 2026
Supervisor: Dr Mushfiqul Alam · Mark 82/100

A single fixed feedforward command alleviates some gusts well and others badly. I developed a gust-conditioned feedforward controller whose elevator and spoiler commands adapt to the gust encounter and to the aircraft's fuel state, and validated it in nonlinear simulation.

An offline optimiser acts as the teacher and a small neural network as the student: the network learns the optimal command map, is frozen, and is then tested against baselines in the nonlinear Simulink model with the feedback loop both open and closed.

MATLABSimulinkOptimization Toolbox · fminconDeep Learning Toolbox
  1. Offline constrained optimiserfmincon generates elevator and spoiler command histories for each gust and fuel case, with a channel-normalised, weighted peak objective.
  2. Training labelsOptimal commands over fuel states and gust lengths are solved once, offline.
  3. Supervised networkA small network learns the command map from three scheduling inputs.
  4. Command lawsThe network's outputs become bounded elevator and spoiler histories.
  5. Linear design evaluationA Pareto family is compared and the operating point is selected and frozen.
  6. Nonlinear validationThe frozen controller is tested in nonlinear Simulink with the feedback loop open and closed.

Flying Qualities & Flight Control

Gain-scheduled pitch CSAS for the Aero L-159 ALCA

A longitudinal command and stability augmentation system designed over four flight conditions of increasing dynamic pressure, then checked against handling-qualities criteria.

Method and figures
  • Open-loop analysis of the actuator-augmented longitudinal model in each flight condition.
  • Pitch-rate feedback gain chosen by root locus and scheduled linearly with dynamic pressure.
  • Reduced-order equivalent model checked against the full-order model in time and frequency.
  • Command pre-filter designed with the dropback criterion for pitch tracking.
  • Stick-force-per-g gearing against the Level 1 envelope, and PIO assessment with the bandwidth and Gibson criteria.
Air-Vehicle Modelling & Simulation

Nonlinear 6-DoF Saab 340B simulation, checked against flight test

I completed a nonlinear six-degree-of-freedom Simulink model of the Saab 340B, trimmed and linearised it, and compared linear, nonlinear and flight-test responses for every dynamic mode.

Method and figures
  • Implemented the navigation block from scratch and verified the trim condition (constant velocities, rates, angles, airspeed and altitude).
  • Built a control-input injection architecture to replay recorded flight-test inputs.
  • Linearised the model, decoupled the longitudinal and lateral-directional state matrices and ran modal analysis.
  • Validated short period, phugoid, Dutch roll, roll and spiral modes against flight data, using time-ratio and logarithmic-decrement analysis.
  • Tracked eigenvalue migration across the airspeed range and studied open-loop Bode responses.
Aircraft System Identification

System identification of the Saab 340B from flight data

Longitudinal and lateral-directional models of the Saab 340B identified from 50 Hz flight-test data, from data compatibility checks through Equation-Error and Output-Error estimation to validation on independent manoeuvres.

Method and figures
  • Kinematic consistency checks with sensor bias and scale-factor correction for every manoeuvre.
  • Transfer-function fits in the frequency and time domains.
  • Equation-Error regression of the non-dimensional aerodynamic coefficients.
  • 3-DoF Output-Error models with residual analysis.
  • Validation on manoeuvres not used for identification (Dutch roll to roll mode, Short Period 2 to Short Period 1).
Multivariable Control Systems

Multivariable and robust control: LQR, LQG, H∞ and μ

State-space and robust control on aircraft and helicopter models: modal analysis, LQR and LQG for a wing-leveller, an H∞ mixed-sensitivity design for a Bell 205 helicopter, and μ-analysis of robustness to uncertainty.

Method and figures
  • Modal analysis and mode isolation from the state matrix.
  • LQR design with pole migration as the control weighting changes, and reference precompensation for a wing-leveller.
  • LQG design with estimator tuning through the noise covariances.
  • H∞ mixed-sensitivity (S/KS) design for the Bell 205, with weight relaxation for an improved design.
  • μ upper and lower bounds for unstructured and structured uncertainty.
Flight Experimental MethodsTeam project

Flight-test analysis: lateral-directional stability of the Saab 340B

Analysis of flight-test data from Cranfield's flying laboratory. My part covered lateral and directional static stability, the Dutch roll and the roll mode, with measured damping and time constants compared against theoretical predictions.

Method and figures

Team report (five authors). The work below is the part I was responsible for.

  • Steady heading sideslip analysis: rudder and aileron gradients against sideslip, effect of speed and configuration, and the CS-25.177 requirement.
  • Dutch roll: logarithmic-decrement analysis of rudder doublets between about 140 and 190 KIAS, with the damping product compared to theory.
  • Roll mode: time-constant estimation from aileron inputs, compared to theory.
Flight Dynamics Principles

From datapack to flying qualities: a twin-turboprop model

A complete linear flight dynamics model built from an aircraft datapack, assessed against Def Stan 00-970 flying qualities and finished with a pitch damper designed by root locus.

Method
  • Mass properties by the discrete element method.
  • Nonlinear 6-DoF equations of motion, small-perturbation linearisation and longitudinal/lateral decoupling.
  • Trim, stability and control derivatives (including propeller effects), state-space matrices and transfer functions.
  • Modal characterisation against Def Stan 00-970 Category B, then a pitch damper by reduced-order root locus.
Rotorcraft Performance, Stability & Control

Mission performance of a tilt-rotor eVTOL concept

Performance and energy analysis of the V-23 SkySpark, an eight-propeller tilt-rotor eVTOL concept, across a seven-phase mission from vertical take-off to landing.

Method and figures
  • Thrust, drag and power in each phase, with momentum theory for hover and climb and a hybrid model for transition.
  • Battery sizing from integrated mission energy, including a 45-minute reserve.
  • Induced velocity and power in take-off, hover and early transition.
  • Sensitivity of battery mass to cruise altitude, cruise speed and payload, and a comparison with current eVTOL programmes.

Wind-tunnel testing: aerofoil pressures and a double-delta wing

Low-speed wind-tunnel labs: tunnel and transducer calibration, pressure distributions and integrated loads on a NACA 23012 aerofoil, and force-balance tests with oil-flow visualisation of the vortex system over a 76°/40° double-delta wing.

Method and figures

Control Systems

Classical control design for a SISO plant

Root locus, Routh–Hurwitz and frequency-domain design, robustness to plant uncertainty, and PID tuning compared across hand tuning, a genetic algorithm and fmincon.

Method and figures
  • Proportional design by root locus within a damping and settling-time region of the s-plane.
  • Bode, Nyquist and Nichols analysis of the compensated loop, bandwidth and sensitivity.
  • PD and state-feedback designs, including disturbance rejection.
  • Multiplicative uncertainty and the small-gain theorem.
  • PID tuning by Ziegler–Nichols, genetic algorithm and fmincon, plus a derivative-on-feedback and pre-filter redesign in SISO Tool.
